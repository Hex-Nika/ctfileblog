Want to learn how to animate but don't know which blocks to use in Scratch?



Well, look no further! I know a lot about using the tweening extensions, and I can guide you through basic functions, systems, etc. Now let's get into it!



# **The Basics**

**The tweening extension has a ton of functions. I'll go over these functions for you to have a better understanding!**

> **Tween (X or Y) Position: This part is used to tell the sprite which way to go**
>
> **X: (Number) Y: (Number): This is used to tell the sprite where to go exactly on the Scratch Loader Viewer**
>
> **Over (Number) Seconds: This shows how long the sprite takes to go from Point A to Point B**
>
> **(Linear, Sine, Quad, expo etc.): These are the crucial tweening fuctions to REALLY animate your sprite.**
>
> **Ease (In, Out, In & Out): This is used as a form of sliding for the sprite to slide left or right.**



**Now that we went over the Basics, you now have a basic understanding of the Tweening Extension**

**If you're trying to go for cool effects for your project, we have the Advanced Tweening Section to level up your animation skills!**

# **Advanced Tweening**

> **1. Try to Map out where you want your sprite to go first before setting up the tweening if you're trying to get it from Point A to Point B**
>
> **2.  For smooth animation meme-like effects, use either the bounce, elastic, back or expo functions to achieve this.**
>
> **3. If you are making an animation that uses a slide in from any direct, its highly recommended that you use the ease out slide.**
>
> **4. If you are trying to achieve a "Head Bop" movement style with the Tweening extenstion, It's highly recommended that you use a wait block in between 2 tweening blocks so the sprite can go back and forth.**
>
> **5. Its very crucial that you keep the tweening time below 1 second for quick slides and/or transitions.**
>
> **6. Make sure to split up your sprites into different pieces so it wont look so static and still.**
